**Title (117 caracteres):** ACCESS TO SPECIALIZED DENTAL CARE FOR PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES IN SÃO PAULO: PUBLIC DATA AND ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

**Authors:** Pedro Amaral*¹; Francisco Perinni²

¹ [filiação]  ² [filiação]

**Abstract (1.540 caracteres):**

Introduction: In the Brazilian Unified Health System (SUS), dental care for persons with disabilities is organized into primary care, Dental Specialty Centers (CEO) and hospital treatment under general anesthesia, yet families struggle to find a service able to solve each case. Objective: To map the registered and delivered supply of specialized dental care for persons with disabilities in the city of São Paulo and relate it to the population with disabilities. Methods: Ecological descriptive study using public data: CEO accreditations and Care Network for Persons with Disabilities (RCPD) incentives from the national health facility registry (CNES, Sep 2026), hospital admissions for dental treatment of patients with special needs from the Hospital Information System (Sep 2025-Aug 2026), and the 2022 Census. Artificial intelligence tools supported data collection, integration and inconsistency detection, and CEO counts were checked against official Ministry of Health data. Results: The city had 30 CEO, matching the official count, 28 of them in the RCPD, for 719,261 persons with disabilities (42,329 aged 2-14 years), or 25,688 per RCPD-CEO. Treatment under general anesthesia was provided in 19 hospitals, but 7 accounted for 80% of 1,114 admissions. Conclusions: Specialized care exists but is concentrated and poorly visible to families. Integrating public data with artificial intelligence was feasible and will support a platform combining a questionnaire and teledentistry to guide families to the appropriate service.
