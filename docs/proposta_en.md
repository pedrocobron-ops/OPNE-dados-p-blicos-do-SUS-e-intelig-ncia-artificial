# Project proposal (English)

**Title:** Digital platform guiding families of persons with disabilities to dental care in the Brazilian Unified Health System (SUS)

**Authors:** Pedro Amaral; Francisco Perinni

The Brazilian Unified Health System (SUS) guarantees dental care for persons with disabilities, but finding a service able to provide it is a challenge for families. This care is organized into levels: primary care units, Dental Specialty Centers (CEO) and tertiary care, which includes hospital treatment under general anesthesia, indicated when the patient cannot be treated in the dental chair. The path to treatment is marked by repeated attempts, and the barriers add up: not knowing where to look, not finding an open door, or reaching a service that does not perform the required procedure.

A preliminary survey in the city of São Paulo, which integrated public data from the national health facility registry (CNES), the Hospital Information System (SIH/SUS) and the 2022 Census with the support of artificial intelligence, measured this scenario. The city has 719,261 persons with disabilities, of whom 42,329 are children aged 2 to 14, and 28 CEO participating in the Care Network for Persons with Disabilities, an average of 25,688 persons with disabilities per center. Treatment under general anesthesia was provided in 19 hospitals, but 80% of the 1,114 admissions in the last year were concentrated in only 7 of them. Resolutive care exists, but it is concentrated and poorly visible to families.

The project proposes a free digital platform for prevention and guidance for parents and caregivers. The caregiver completes a simple questionnaire, developed by public-sector dentists, about the patient's needs. Next, a teleconsultation with professionals registered on the platform deepens the understanding of the case and, based on the service mapping, directs the family to the appropriate level of care and facility, explaining the SUS referral pathway. The platform also offers content on oral care at home, and an artificial intelligence assistant will answer questions in plain language, using only validated information. The teleconsultation provides guidance but does not replace in-person assessment.

Development will begin in São Paulo, with tests on simulated cases and, after approval by a Research Ethics Committee, evaluation with families, in compliance with Brazil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD). Since the databases used are national, the model can be expanded to the state and replicated in any municipality in the country at low cost. More than mapping services, the project turns scattered public data into concrete guidance, so that no family navigates the health system without an answer and every patient reaches the treatment they need sooner.

**Keywords:** Persons with Disabilities; Dental Care; Health Information Systems; Artificial Intelligence; Health Services Accessibility.
