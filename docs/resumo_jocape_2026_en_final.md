# Resumo JoCAPE 2026 — versão final (revisão da orientadora)

Normas: até 1.600 caracteres com espaços; título em MAIÚSCULAS até 120 caracteres; orientador(a) como último autor.

## Versão para submissão (ajustada ao limite — 1.595 caracteres)

Introduction: Although the SUS guarantees dental care for people with disabilities (PWD), access to appropriate services remains challenging. Families struggle to identify services and navigate referral pathways across primary, secondary, and tertiary care. Objective: To develop a free digital platform integrating public health data, teleconsultation, and artificial intelligence (AI) to guide families of PWD to appropriate dental care within the SUS. Methods: A preliminary assessment of São Paulo integrated data from CNES, SIH/SUS, and the 2022 Census, supported by AI. The platform will include a caregiver questionnaire, teleconsultation with registered dentists, a georeferenced service directory, oral health education, and an AI assistant providing evidence-based answers. Development will begin with simulated case testing and, after Research Ethics Committee approval, evaluation with families, in compliance with LGPD. Results: São Paulo has 719,261 PWD, including 42,329 children aged 2–14 years, and 28 dental specialty centers (CEO) affiliated with the Care Network for People with Disabilities, averaging 25,688 PWD per center. General anesthesia dental treatment was provided in 19 hospitals; however, 80% of 1,114 hospitalizations in the past year occurred in only seven hospitals, revealing high concentration. Conclusions: The platform will transform fragmented public data into accessible guidance, helping families identify appropriate services and referral pathways. Using national databases, the model can be expanded statewide and replicated across Brazil at low cost.

## Texto original da orientadora (1.681 caracteres — acima do limite)

Introduction: Although the SUS guarantees dental care for people with disabilities (PWD), access to appropriate services remains challenging. Families often face difficulties identifying services and navigating referral pathways across primary, secondary, and tertiary care. Objective: To develop a free digital platform integrating public health data, teleconsultation, and artificial intelligence (AI) to guide families of PWD to appropriate dental care within the SUS. Methods: A preliminary assessment of São Paulo integrated data from CNES, SIH/SUS, and the 2022 Census, supported by AI. The platform will include a caregiver questionnaire, teleconsultation with registered dentists, a georeferenced service directory, oral health education, and an AI assistant providing evidence-based answers. Development will begin in São Paulo, followed by simulated case testing and, after Research Ethics Committee approval, evaluation with families, in compliance with LGPD. Results: São Paulo has 719,261 PWD, including 42,329 children aged 2–14 years, and 28 dental specialty centers (CEO) affiliated with the Care Network for People with Disabilities, averaging 25,688 PWD per center. General anesthesia dental treatment was provided in 19 hospitals; however, 80% of 1,114 hospitalizations in the previous year occurred in only seven hospitals, revealing a highly concentrated service network. Conclusions: The platform will transform fragmented public data into accessible guidance, helping families identify appropriate services and referral pathways. Using nationally available databases, the model has potential for statewide expansion and replication across Brazil at low cost.
